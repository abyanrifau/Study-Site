## The three measures

<Figure caption="The same workforce arranged two ways. Tall means many layers and close supervision; flat means fewer layers and more delegation.">
  <OrgStructureDiagram />
</Figure>

- **Hierarchy** — how many levels there are.
- **Chain of command** — the route an instruction travels from top to bottom. More layers, longer chain.
- **Span of control** — how many people report to one manager. Wide spans force delegation; narrow spans allow close supervision.

Note the relationship: for a given number of employees, **wider spans mean fewer layers**. You cannot have both a wide span and a tall structure in the same organisation of a fixed size. Questions test whether you understand that trade-off.

## Tall against flat

| | Tall | Flat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layers | many | few |
| Span of control | narrow | wide |
| Supervision | close | limited, so delegation is necessary |
| Communication | slow, distorted by the number of layers | fast and direct |
| Promotion prospects | clear, many rungs | few, so people leave to progress |
| Cost | high, many managers to pay | lower |
| Suits | large, safety-critical or highly standardised work | small businesses, creative and fast-moving work |

## Matrix structure

Employees are grouped into project teams drawn from different functions, and report to both a project leader and their functional manager.

**Good for:** bringing the right specialists together, sharing knowledge across functions, flexibility on projects.

**Bad for:** clarity. Two bosses means conflicting priorities, more meetings, and confusion about who decides. It works where projects genuinely need cross-functional teams and the culture supports it.

## Centralised or decentralised

**Centralised** — decisions at the top. Consistent, tight control, quick in a crisis, and it uses the experience of senior managers. It also creates bottlenecks, ignores local knowledge and demotivates the people below.

**Decentralised** — authority delegated down. Faster local decisions, better use of local knowledge, and strongly motivating because it gives people responsibility, which is a Herzberg motivator. It risks inconsistency, needs capable and trained staff, and makes control harder.

The right answer depends on the business: a bank's lending rules should be centralised; a resort's response to a guest complaint should not.

## Effect on efficiency and motivation

This is what the specification actually asks you to discuss.

**Efficiency.** Fewer layers means faster decisions, less distortion in communication and a lower management wage bill. Too flat, though, and managers are stretched so thin that nothing gets supervised properly and mistakes rise.

**Motivation.** Flat and decentralised gives responsibility, involvement and empowerment, all of which Herzberg identifies as genuine motivators. But flat structures offer few promotions, so ambitious people leave, and wide spans mean less support from a manager who has twenty other reports.

<RealWorld where="Across countries">

**Delayering.** Large firms across Europe and North America removed middle management layers through the 1990s and 2000s to cut cost and speed up decisions. The saving was immediate; the loss of the informal knowledge and mentoring those managers provided took years to become visible.

**Decentralisation in practice.** International hotel groups typically centralise brand standards, pricing systems and procurement, while decentralising day-to-day guest decisions to the property. It is the same organisation being centralised and decentralised at once, on different things, which is a useful point in an evaluation.

</RealWorld>

<Tip>

**How this topic is tested.** Define (2) on span of control, chain of command, decentralisation; Explain (4) on one effect of delayering; Assess (10) on whether a business should flatten its structure or decentralise.

**A reliable structure for the Assess:**

1. Describe the current structure using the source. Count the layers or the span if given.
2. Argue the efficiency effect, both ways.
3. Argue the motivation effect, both ways.
4. Judge on what this business needs: speed, or control.

**Getting evaluation marks:**

- **It depends on the capability of the staff below.** Decentralisation without competent people is just lost control.
- **It depends on the type of work.** Safety-critical or highly regulated work needs supervision; creative work does not.
- **You can centralise some things and decentralise others.** Very few candidates say this, and it is the most sophisticated point available.
- **Delayering saves wages and loses experience.** Name both.

</Tip>

<Mistakes>

- **Confusing chain of command with span of control.** Chain is vertical (how many layers an instruction passes through); span is horizontal (how many report to one manager).
- **Saying a structure is both tall and wide-spanned.** For a given size, wider spans mean fewer layers.
- **Assuming flat is better.** It stretches managers and removes promotion paths.
- **Confusing delegation with abdication.** Delegating responsibility does not remove the manager's accountability.
- **Treating decentralisation as all-or-nothing.** Most businesses do both, on different decisions.
- **Forgetting the motivation link.** Responsibility and involvement are Herzberg motivators, so structure connects directly to 1.3.4.4.
- **Describing a matrix structure without the two-bosses problem.** That conflict is the main drawback.

</Mistakes>
