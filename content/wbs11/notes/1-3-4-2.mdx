## Internal against external

| | Internal | External |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | low, no advertising or agency fees | higher: advertising, agencies, interview time |
| Speed | fast, the person is already there | slow, notice periods and onboarding |
| Knowledge of the business | already has it | has to learn it |
| New ideas | none, the culture is reproduced | fresh thinking and outside experience |
| Motivation | strong: promotion shows there is a path | can demotivate those passed over |
| Risk | low, their work is known | higher, an interview reveals less than you think |
| Side effect | creates another vacancy below | brings in skills the business lacks |

The trade-off in one line: **internal is cheaper, faster and safer; external is the only way to get skills and ideas the business does not already have.**

## The costs of getting it wrong

Recruitment, selection and training all cost money, and the specification asks you to know that.

- **Direct costs:** advertising, agency fees, management time interviewing, induction, training.
- **Indirect costs:** the vacancy period when work is not done, the learning period when the new person is slower, and the cost of the whole process again if they leave.

That is why **labour turnover** matters financially, not just as a wellbeing issue. Every leaver triggers the full cost cycle again, which is the strongest argument for treating staff as an asset (**1.3.4.1**).

## The three types of training

<Chunk title="Induction" open>

Given when someone joins: the business, the role, the systems, health and safety, who is who.

Cheap and short, and it reduces early mistakes and early leavers, because most people who quit in the first month do so because they were left to sink. Skipping it is a false economy.

</Chunk>

<Chunk title="On-the-job">

Learning while doing, usually alongside an experienced colleague.

**For:** cheap, immediately relevant, productive while learning, no travel.

**Against:** the trainer is not producing while training, bad habits get passed on, and it teaches only what this business already does.

</Chunk>

<Chunk title="Off-the-job">

A course, a qualification, an external provider.

**For:** access to expertise the business does not have, recognised qualifications, no distraction from the workplace, and it can bring in new methods.

**Against:** expensive, the employee is not working while training, it may not fit the business's own procedures, and it makes the employee more attractive to competitors.

</Chunk>

**The last point is the standard evaluation line:** training raises productivity and can also raise the chance that a better-qualified employee leaves. The counter is that good training is itself a reason to stay, so poorly paid trained staff leave while well treated trained staff do not. It is the combination that matters.

<RealWorld where="Maldives">

A resort recruiting a dive instructor has to recruit externally, because the qualification takes years and cannot be created internally. Recruiting a front desk supervisor is usually internal, because knowing the property and the guests matters more than anything an outsider brings.

The training economics are unusually stark on a remote island. Off-the-job training means flying someone to Male or abroad, which is far more expensive than in a city, so most training is on-the-job and multi-skilled. And because replacing a departing instructor mid-season may mean leaving a role unfilled for weeks, retention is worth more there than in a place where a replacement can start on Monday.

</RealWorld>

<Tip>

**How this topic is tested.** Define (2); Explain (4) on one advantage of internal or external recruitment, or one drawback of a training type; Assess (10) on whether a business should recruit internally or externally, or invest in training.

**Answering "internal or external?"** Decide what the vacancy actually needs:

- Needs skills or ideas the business lacks → external.
- Needs knowledge of the business, and speed and cost matter → internal.
- Then note the side effect: internal fills this role and opens another.

**Getting evaluation marks:**

- **It depends on the skill.** Some skills genuinely cannot be grown internally in the time available.
- **It depends on the labour market.** Where skilled staff are scarce or the location is remote, external recruitment may not be realistic at all.
- **Trained staff become more employable elsewhere**, so training and retention have to be handled together.
- **Use the numbers.** If turnover or recruitment cost figures are given, calculate the cost of replacing someone and compare it with the cost of keeping them.

</Tip>

<Mistakes>

- **Confusing recruitment and selection.** Recruitment attracts applicants; selection chooses between them.
- **Saying internal recruitment has no cost.** There is still management time, training, and the vacancy it creates below.
- **Forgetting induction is training.** It is the cheapest training with the clearest return.
- **Treating on-the-job training as free.** The trainer stops producing while they train.
- **Claiming training always reduces turnover.** It can increase it if pay does not follow the new skills.
- **Ignoring the opportunity cost of the vacancy period** while the role is unfilled.
- **Not applying to the business in the source.** A remote location, a scarce skill or a high turnover figure should change the answer.

</Mistakes>
