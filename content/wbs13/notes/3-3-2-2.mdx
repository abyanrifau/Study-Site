## How businesses grow organically

- **Sell more to existing customers.** Promotion, loyalty schemes, wider distribution. This is Ansoff's market penetration.
- **Develop new products** for the customers you have.
- **Enter new markets** with the existing product, including new countries.
- **Open more outlets**, branches or sites.
- **Invest in capacity** so more can be produced.
- **Franchise**, which expands the brand using other people's capital while the franchisor keeps control of the model.

Franchising is worth noting specifically, because it is organic growth for the franchisor that does not require the franchisor's own capital. It is the closest thing to fast, cheap organic growth, and the cost is reliance on franchisees to maintain standards.

## Advantages

- **Lower risk.** Growth happens in increments, so mistakes are small and correctable. There is no gamble on valuing another business correctly.
- **Cheaper.** Can often be funded from retained profit, avoiding interest and avoiding new shareholders.
- **Control retained.** No dilution of ownership, and the founder keeps decision-making.
- **Culture preserved.** No two-culture integration problem, which is the most common cause of acquisition failure.
- **Managers grow with the business.** Existing staff develop capability as the business scales, and promotion prospects motivate them.
- **Easier to manage.** The pace is one the existing management team can absorb.

## Disadvantages

- **Slow.** In a fast-growing market, slow growth means losing share to faster competitors.
- **Limited by internal resources.** You can only grow as fast as retained profit and management capacity allow.
- **May be limited by the market.** If the existing market is saturated, penetration cannot deliver much.
- **Competitors can acquire their way to scale** and reach economies of scale before you do.
- **Does not bring in new capability.** An acquisition buys skills, patents, brands and market access instantly; organic growth has to build them.

## Financing organic growth

Usually retained profit, which is why organic growth and profitability are linked: an unprofitable business cannot grow organically because there is nothing to reinvest.

Where retained profit is insufficient, a loan or owner's capital extends the pace, and the question then becomes whether the cash flow can service the repayments. That links to WBS12 **2.3.1.3**.

**The opportunity cost matters here.** Retained profit used for growth is not available as a cash buffer or as dividends. A business that reinvests everything is growing and fragile.

<RealWorld where="Maldives">

A guesthouse growing organically has a clear sequence: fill existing rooms through better reviews and marketing, then add services for the same guests, diving, excursions, food, then add rooms, then perhaps a second property funded by accumulated profit.

Each step is affordable, reversible and within the owner's capacity to manage. The constraint is time: a competitor with outside capital could buy three properties in a year while this operator adds four rooms.

Whether that matters depends on whether the market is growing fast enough to punish slowness. In a market with limited total demand, being the best small operator can be a better outcome than being a rushed medium one.

</RealWorld>

<Tip>

**How this topic is tested.** Define (2); Explain (4) on one advantage; Assess (12) or Evaluate (20) comparing organic with inorganic growth.

**The comparison answer:**

1. **Establish the business's position** from the source: cash, management capacity, market growth rate, competitive pressure.
2. **Argue organic** on risk, cost, control and culture, applied to this business.
3. **Argue inorganic** on speed and capability, applied to this business.
4. **Identify the decisive factor.** Usually it is either how fast the market is moving or whether the business could finance an acquisition at all.
5. Judge, and consider a sequence rather than a choice.

**Getting evaluation marks:**

- **Speed is the real trade-off.** Organic growth is safer and slower, and slow can be fatal in a fast market.
- **Growth is limited by management capacity**, not just cash. This is the entrepreneur-to-leader problem from WBS11 **1.3.4.5**.
- **Retained profit has an opportunity cost.**
- **Organic growth cannot buy capability** the business does not have.

</Tip>

<Mistakes>

- **Saying organic growth is "always safer" and stopping.** Develop why, and note the cost of being slow.
- **Forgetting it is limited by retained profit and management capacity.**
- **Treating franchising as inorganic growth.** For the franchisor it expands the brand organically, using franchisees' capital.
- **Ignoring market saturation.** Penetration cannot grow a full market.
- **Assuming organic growth needs no finance.** It needs cash, just usually less of it.
- **Not applying to the source.** A business in a fast-growing market and one in a static market need different answers.

</Mistakes>
