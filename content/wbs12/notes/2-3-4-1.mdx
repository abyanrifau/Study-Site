## The four methods of production

| Method | What it is | Suits | Unit cost | Flexibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Job** | one item at a time, to order | bespoke work, tailoring, construction | high | very high |
| **Batch** | groups of identical items | bakeries, paint, clothing sizes | medium | medium |
| **Flow** | continuous line, standardised | cars, bottled drinks, electronics | low | very low |
| **Cell** | teams each own a whole section | mixed, often used with flow | medium | medium |

**The trade-off is cost against flexibility.** Job production gives the customer exactly what they want at a high price. Flow production gives a very low unit cost and cannot easily change what it makes.

**Cell production** is worth understanding properly, because it is the one students describe vaguely. Rather than each worker repeating one task along a line, a team takes responsibility for a complete stage. That raises motivation, because the work is more varied and the team owns an identifiable output, which is job enrichment in Herzberg's terms. It also improves quality, because the team can see and fix its own problems.

## Productivity

<Formula name="Labour productivity" note="Output per worker. Rising productivity means the same people produce more, so the labour cost per unit falls.">
Labour productivity = Total output ÷ Number of employees
</Formula>

<Worked>

A workshop produces **7,200** units a month with **24** employees.

`Labour productivity = 7,200 ÷ 24 = 300 units per employee per month`

After training and a new machine, output rises to **9,000** with the same 24 staff.

`New productivity = 9,000 ÷ 24 = 375 units per employee per month`

That is a **25% rise in productivity**.

**Now the point that earns the marks.** If labour costs are $48,000 a month either way:

`Labour cost per unit before = $48,000 ÷ 7,200 = $6.67`

`Labour cost per unit after  = $48,000 ÷ 9,000 = $5.33`

Unit labour cost fell by $1.34, about 20%. **That** is why productivity matters: it feeds straight into unit cost, and therefore into either a lower price or a wider margin.

</Worked>

**What influences productivity:** training and skill, motivation, technology and equipment, quality of management, working conditions, and the production method itself.

**Ways to improve it:** train staff, invest in better equipment, improve motivation (see WBS11 **1.3.4.4**), reorganise the workflow, introduce cell production, reduce downtime and waste.

Each costs money, and the judgement is whether the unit cost saving exceeds the cost of achieving it.

## Efficiency

Efficiency means producing at the **minimum average cost**. It is related to productivity but not the same: productivity is about output per input, efficiency is about cost per unit.

You can raise productivity and become less efficient, if the equipment that raised it cost more than the labour it saved. Making that distinction is a strong exam point.

**What influences efficiency:** capacity utilisation (**2.3.4.2**), economies of scale, waste levels, the production method, and standardisation.

## Labour or capital intensive

**Labour-intensive** — mostly people. Lower set-up cost, flexible, can be scaled up and down, and it is vulnerable to wage rises, absence and turnover, and quality varies between individuals.

**Capital-intensive** — mostly machinery. Consistent quality, low unit cost at volume, works continuously, and it needs heavy upfront investment, is inflexible, and a breakdown stops everything.

The right choice depends on **wage levels, the volume produced, and how standardised the product is**. It is also why the same product is made labour-intensively in one country and capital-intensively in another.

## Lead-in time as an advantage

A business that can develop and launch a product faster than its rivals reaches the market first, sets the standard, earns at a premium before competitors arrive, and responds sooner when tastes change. Short lead-in time is therefore a competitive advantage in its own right, not just an operational detail.

<RealWorld where="Maldives">

A guesthouse is inherently **labour-intensive** and closest to **job production**: each guest gets an individually handled stay rather than a standardised output. That is why unit costs are high and why service quality, and therefore staff, matter so much.

The productivity measure that actually matters is not units per worker but something like room nights serviced per staff member. Multi-skilling raises it directly, because one person covering reception, breakfast and housekeeping across a quiet period does the work that three narrowly-trained staff would otherwise be needed for. That is why small properties multi-skill as a matter of course.

</RealWorld>

<Tip>

**How this topic is tested.** Calculate (4) for labour productivity, Explain (4) on a production method or a way to raise productivity, Assess (10) on whether to change production method or invest in equipment.

**On the calculation:** divide output by workers, state the unit ("units per worker per month"), and then take the extra step of working out the effect on unit cost. That second step is what separates a 2-mark answer from a 4-mark one.

**Getting evaluation marks:**

- **Productivity and efficiency are not the same.** New equipment can raise one and lower the other.
- **Investment has to pay for itself.** Compare the unit cost saving against the cost of the machine.
- **Flexibility has value.** Flow production is cheapest per unit and cannot respond to a change in demand.
- **Capital intensity suits high volume and high wages.** Labour intensity suits variable demand and low wages.
- **People are not machines.** Pushing productivity too hard raises errors, absence and turnover.

</Tip>

<Mistakes>

- **Confusing productivity with production.** Production is total output; productivity is output per input.
- **Confusing productivity with efficiency.** Efficiency is about minimum average cost.
- **Recommending flow production for a bespoke product.** It cannot handle variety.
- **Saying capital-intensive is always cheaper.** Only at sufficient volume, and it is inflexible.
- **Forgetting the cost of raising productivity.** Training and equipment are not free.
- **Describing cell production as just teamwork.** The team owns a complete stage, which is what produces the motivation and quality benefits.
- **Leaving out units** in the productivity answer.

</Mistakes>
